You are working on AfroTune AI.

Before treating this as a coding task, understand the product.

AfroTune is a WhatsApp-first AI music creation platform. The customer is not coming to configure an AI model or fill out a technical form. They are coming with a human idea, story, person, occasion, emotion or musical direction and expect AfroTune to turn that into a finished personalized song.

The core promise is simple:

A user should be able to message AfroTune on WhatsApp and say something like:

“Make a birthday song for my wife Ada. We’ve been married for seven years. She loves Afrobeats. Make it romantic but still something she can dance to, and put some Yoruba inside.”

AfroTune should understand that naturally, extract what it already knows, ask only for genuinely missing information, arrive at a clear internal Song Brief, show the customer the song direction and price, collect payment or use existing credits, generate the music asynchronously, process and store the audio, then send the finished song back through WhatsApp with lyrics, download/share options and a rating experience.

The product should feel like talking to an intelligent music producer, not completing a form.

==================================================
THE HEART OF AFROTUNE
==================================================

WhatsApp is the interface, not the product.

The actual heart of AfroTune is the transformation:

Human story/request
→ conversational understanding
→ structured Song Brief
→ African/cultural/music intelligence
→ composition direction
→ music generation
→ finished personalized song.

AfroTune should own the understanding and composition logic.

The external music provider should only be responsible for rendering the resulting music.

The system should never reduce itself to:

WhatsApp → raw user prompt → music API.

The value of AfroTune is what happens between the customer's story and the music model.

==================================================
CORE PRODUCT EXPERIENCE
==================================================

The primary experience happens inside WhatsApp.

The user should be free to speak naturally.

Example:

User:
“I need a wedding song for Tolu and Femi. They met at university, they’ve been together for six years. Make it Afrobeats with some Highlife influence and mostly English.”

AfroTune should understand as much as possible without asking redundant questions.

Internally it may form:

occasion: wedding
recipients: Tolu and Femi
relationship/story: met at university, together six years
genre: Afrobeats
secondary_style: Highlife
language: English
mood: unknown
voice: unknown

AfroTune then asks only useful follow-up questions such as:

“Do you want it emotional and romantic or more celebratory and danceable?”

The customer should never have to manually answer information AfroTune has already understood.

Structured buttons, WhatsApp Flows and small web views are allowed where they improve the experience, but they are secondary to conversation.

Useful structured interactions may include:

- male / female / surprise-me vocal selection
- choosing a credit package
- confirming a song
- buying credits
- rating a song
- selecting between a few options when conversation would be unnecessarily slow.

There should also be an optional “choose everything myself” path in the future, but natural conversation is the main experience.

==================================================
SONG BRIEF
==================================================

Every song creation should ultimately produce a structured internal Song Brief.

The exact schema can evolve based on the existing database, but conceptually it represents:

- user/customer
- country or cultural context
- occasion
- recipient name or names
- relationship
- story and personal details
- genre
- genre variation/fusion
- language or languages
- mood
- vocal preference
- relevant music/cultural context
- target duration
- any required phrases/names
- price
- generation status
- provider configuration
- final lyrics
- final audio asset
- generation metadata.

The Song Brief is owned by AfroTune and should not be coupled to the schema of one music provider.

==================================================
COUNTRIES, GENRES AND LANGUAGES
==================================================

The validation market starts with Nigeria, and the existing database already contains Nigerian country, genre, language, instrument and music relationship data.

However, the application must not be architected as “Nigeria software.”

Countries, languages, genres, genre variations, instruments, cultural information and pricing rules should be data-driven.

Adding Cameroon, Ghana, Kenya or another market later should primarily involve new validated data and configuration rather than rewriting the core product.

Do not hard-code country-specific product logic unnecessarily.

The current Nigerian genre foundation includes areas such as:

- Afrobeats
- Highlife
- Fuji
- Juju
- Apala

Use the existing Supabase data as the starting source of truth.

Do not invent cultural information.

==================================================
MUSIC INTELLIGENCE
==================================================

The long-term differentiator is AfroTune’s African Music Intelligence layer.

For this validation build, this does not mean training a proprietary music model.

It means using AfroTune’s curated data and product logic to make the generated song more culturally and musically informed.

Conceptually:

Song Brief
+
country data
+
genre data
+
instruments
+
rhythm/style characteristics
+
language context
+
occasion
+
mood
+
personal story
=
music/composition specification.

This specification then becomes the input to the actual music-generation provider.

The intelligence layer should remain under AfroTune’s control so that it can improve independently of whichever provider generates audio.

==================================================
MUSIC GENERATION
==================================================

The intended Phase 1 provider is ElevenLabs Music, subject to practical quality testing with AfroTune-style songs.

Do not architect the system around ElevenLabs directly.

There should be a music-provider abstraction so AfroTune talks to something conceptually like:

MusicGenerationProvider

with responsibilities such as:

generate
retrieve/result
estimate cost
expose capabilities
normalize provider errors.

ElevenLabs is the first implementation of that contract.

Another provider should be replaceable later without rewriting the conversation system, payments, database or customer experience.

The provider needs to be tested particularly for:

- Afrobeats
- Highlife
- Fuji
- Juju
- Apala
- Nigerian names
- English
- Nigerian Pidgin
- Yoruba
- Igbo
- Hausa
- mixed-language songs
- male and female vocals
- birthday
- wedding
- love/romantic songs.

Do not assume that a provider being technically multilingual means every Nigerian language or genre sounds culturally convincing.

Quality should be measured.

There is no lyric-approval stage in the main Phase 1 experience.

The flow is intentionally:

conversation
→ song direction confirmed
→ payment/credits
→ generation
→ finished song.

The customer should experience the magic of describing what they want and receiving the completed result.

Lyrics may still be stored and displayed after generation.

==================================================
AUDIO PROCESSING
==================================================

The generated audio should pass through an internal technical processing layer before delivery.

Use FFmpeg for this layer rather than introducing another paid audio-processing SaaS unless there is a strong engineering reason.

This layer is for technical preparation, not unnecessary creative remixing.

It may handle:

- validating that the generated file is healthy
- inspecting format/codec/duration
- detecting obvious invalid output
- trimming accidental excessive silence where appropriate
- normalizing delivery loudness where necessary
- generating a WhatsApp/download-friendly MP3
- extracting duration and useful metadata
- producing other delivery formats later if required.

Preserve the original provider output.

A song should be able to have both:

original provider asset
and
processed delivery asset.

==================================================
WHATSAPP
==================================================

Meta WhatsApp Cloud API is the primary customer interface.

The system receives messages through a webhook and sends responses through Meta’s API.

A WhatsApp user’s phone number is their primary identity.

Customers should not be required to create a conventional email/password account simply to create music.

The user identity should link to:

- WhatsApp phone number
- profile/basic details
- credit wallet
- credit ledger
- preferences
- songs
- payments
- ratings
- referrals
- conversation/session state
- activity history.

The webhook must remain fast.

Long-running AI/music work must never block the WhatsApp webhook.

Meta credentials and identifiers must never be hard-coded.

Relevant configuration belongs in environment variables, including values such as:

META_APP_ID
META_APP_SECRET
WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN
WHATSAPP_PHONE_NUMBER_ID
WHATSAPP_BUSINESS_ACCOUNT_ID
WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN
WHATSAPP_API_VERSION

If different naming is more appropriate for the implementation, keep names clear and documented, but all sensitive or deployment-specific configuration must remain outside source code.

==================================================
CONVERSATIONAL AI
==================================================

The conversational model is not the authority for financial or database state.

Its job is primarily to:

- understand natural language
- extract song information
- understand what the user means
- decide what information is missing
- produce natural conversational responses
- assist with composition/song direction.

Important product/business state remains deterministic backend logic.

For example, the LLM must not decide whether someone has enough credits simply because the conversation suggests they do.

The backend reads the actual ledger.

The conversational layer should preferably produce structured outputs internally when interpreting the user.

It should be robust to messages such as:

“Do something special for my mum”
“Make it like old Nigerian Highlife”
“Actually change it to Yoruba”
“Make it more emotional”
“Her name is Grace, not Gloria”
“Start over”
“How many credits do I have?”
“I want another one like my last song.”

Conversation state should survive individual webhook requests.

The exact LLM provider should remain configurable.

Environment configuration may include whichever provider is selected, for example:

LLM_PROVIDER
OPENAI_API_KEY
OPENAI_MODEL

or equivalent provider variables.

Do not couple core product behavior unnecessarily to one conversational model.

==================================================
CREDIT WALLET
==================================================

Every regular user has a credit wallet.

A user should not have to go through Paystack every time they create a song.

They can buy credits and keep them for future usage.

Possible customer experience:

1 credit
5 credits
10 credits
20 credits

with pricing controlled from product/database configuration rather than hard-coded permanently.

Paystack tops up the wallet.

Song generation consumes credits.

Credits should not be represented only as:

users.credit_balance = X

The existing ledger architecture should be respected.

Conceptually:

+10 purchased
-1 generation
-1 generation
+1 failed-generation refund
+2 referral reward

The visible balance is derived from ledger activity.

Transactions must be auditable and idempotent.

Generation/payment duplication caused by repeated Meta or Paystack webhooks must not double-charge or double-credit users.

==================================================
PAYSTACK
==================================================

Paystack is the Phase 1 payment provider.

It is used primarily to buy credits.

Flow:

user chooses credit pack
→ Paystack payment initialized
→ user completes payment
→ backend receives/verifies Paystack event
→ payment record becomes confirmed
→ corresponding credit transaction is added
→ wallet balance increases
→ pending song flow can continue.

Never credit a wallet merely because payment initialization succeeded.

Verification happens server-side.

Paystack secrets and configuration must remain in environment variables, including values such as:

PAYSTACK_PUBLIC_KEY
PAYSTACK_SECRET_KEY

Webhook verification must follow Paystack’s actual signing mechanism rather than inventing a separate secret if Paystack does not use one.

==================================================
GENERATION LIFECYCLE
==================================================

Music generation is asynchronous.

Conceptually:

song request created
→ payment/credit confirmed
→ generation job queued
→ worker receives job
→ music intelligence/composition created
→ music provider called
→ provider result received
→ original audio stored
→ FFmpeg processing
→ processed audio stored
→ database updated
→ WhatsApp user notified.

Useful states may include concepts such as:

draft
collecting_details
ready_for_confirmation
awaiting_payment
queued
generating
processing
completed
failed
moderation_required
cancelled.

Use whatever state model best fits the existing database after inspection.

Paid/credited requests must not disappear silently.

Failures should be recorded.

Safe retries should exist.

A generation that genuinely fails should be able to restore the consumed credit according to business rules.

==================================================
QUEUE / WORKER
==================================================

Long-running generation belongs outside the synchronous API/webhook process.

Use a practical queue/worker architecture suitable for an early SaaS product.

Do not introduce unnecessary infrastructure complexity.

Redis or another lightweight reliable mechanism may be used if appropriate.

Queue configuration must be environment-driven, for example:

REDIS_URL

if Redis is selected.

The system should support:

- queued jobs
- retries
- failure recording
- idempotency
- safe concurrency
- recovering jobs that fail unexpectedly.

==================================================
SUPABASE STORAGE
==================================================

Supabase Storage is the initial asset storage layer.

Music should not live as raw database blobs.

The storage design should support assets such as:

original generated audio
processed delivery audio
possibly artwork later
other song-related media if required.

A logical structure could resemble:

songs/{user-or-song-namespace}/{song_id}/original.*
songs/{user-or-song-namespace}/{song_id}/delivery.mp3

but use the storage structure that is secure, maintainable and compatible with the existing project.

Database rows should reference storage objects.

Access policies should prevent users from arbitrarily reading other customers’ private assets.

Use signed/public URLs intentionally based on the actual customer experience.

Relevant configuration is derived from Supabase environment credentials rather than hard-coded bucket URLs.

Bucket names may be configurable through environment variables if appropriate, for example:

SUPABASE_MUSIC_BUCKET

==================================================
PHASE 1 OUTCOME
==================================================

The successful result is not “there is a webhook” or “the dashboard loads.”

The result is this:

A real customer discovers or messages AfroTune on WhatsApp.

They describe the song they want in ordinary language.

AfroTune understands them and has a natural conversation until it has enough information.

It creates an internal Song Brief.

The customer sees a clear summary and price.

If they already have enough credits, they continue immediately.

If they do not, they can buy a credit pack through Paystack.

Their wallet is updated only after verified payment.

The song consumes the appropriate credit.

Generation happens asynchronously.

AfroTune enriches the Song Brief using its music/cultural data.

The music provider generates the track.

The original audio is preserved.

FFmpeg prepares the delivery copy.

The assets are securely stored in Supabase Storage.

The generation record becomes complete.

The customer receives the finished song on WhatsApp.

They can listen, view lyrics, download, rate and share it.

They can return days later, still have unused credits in their wallet, and create another song without being forced through Paystack again.

The founder can open the dashboard and see that customer, their payment, credit activity, song, generation status and rating.

That complete loop is AfroTune Phase 1.

==================================================
ENGINEERING CHARACTER
==================================================

This is a validation-stage commercial product, not a disposable prototype.

It should be simple enough to build and operate economically, while being reliable around money, credits and customer songs.

Avoid overengineering.

Also avoid shortcuts that make payment duplication, lost jobs, leaking service-role credentials, broken credit balances or untraceable generation failures likely.

Use the existing system where it is sound.

Challenge it where it is not.

Do not preserve an architecture merely because an older AI-generated PRD suggested it.

The product should be understandable, testable and maintainable by another competent developer after handoff.

The architecture should reflect the actual product:

conversation
→ Song Brief
→ music intelligence
→ payment/credits
→ asynchronous generation
→ audio processing
→ storage
→ delivery
→ feedback.

That is AfroTune.
